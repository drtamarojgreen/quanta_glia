## Module 1: The Mycelium of Truth (Understanding CDD)

### The Vision
Traditional development focuses on **behavior** (what a plant does). CDD focuses on **structure** (how the trellis is built).

### Why CDD over BDD?
Behavior Driven Development (BDD) assumes the gardener (the developer) understands the "why." AI agents, however, are masters of "what" but often hallucinate the "why."
*   **BDD:** "The plant should turn toward the light." (Abstract, requires intent)
*   **CDD:** "The stem must have exactly three nodes and be anchored to a 45-degree support." (Structural, verifiable)

### Core Philosophy
**Restrict to Empower.** By defining what is *not* allowed, we create a safe zone where growth is guaranteed to be healthy.

## Module 2: The Primitives of the Ecosystem (Learning CDD)

To navigate the greenhouse, you must understand the four organic components of CDD:

### 1. The Situation (The Environment)
The global state of your greenhouse. It defines the tools, versions, and environmental constants (e.g., `python_version = 3.9`, `memory_limit = 2GB`).

### 2. Facts (The Soil)
Small, immutable units of truth stored in the **Fact Registry**.
*Example:* `pruner_logic_operational = true`.
Facts are discovered by probes (Cards) and used by other systems to make decisions.

### 3. Cards (The Probes)
CDD Classes. These are focused, structural probes that verify a single aspect of the environment. Unlike traditional tests, they don't just assert; they **report truth**.
*   **Green Syntax:** A structured format designed for machine reasoning.

### 4. Sips (The Irrigation)
Small Integration Procedures. A "Sip" is a single run of a Card. If a Sip fails, the irrigation stops—preventing the "water" of new code from reaching a faulty "root."

## Module 3: Climate Control (Programming with CDD)

The CDD lifecycle adds a critical phase to the standard SDLC: **The Restrictions Phase.**

### The CDD Lifecycle:
1.  **Requirements:** What does the mind need? (e.g., "Reduce Anxiety")
2.  **Design:** How do we visualize it? (e.g., "Smooth blue gradients, rhythmic motion")
3.  **Restrictions (The Trellis):** Define the boundaries.
    *   `no_complex_loops`
    *   `max_particles: 100`
    *   `tool_required: opensim_utils`
4.  **Coding:** The AI generates code within these pre-defined trellises.
5.  **Maintenance:** Pruning and evolving the environment.

### The Rule of Organic Geometry
Favor organic branching over mechanical rigidity, unless representing maladaptive patterns. Every line of code must have a conceptual justification—no purely decorative elements.

## Module 4: The Harvest of Proof (Verifying Reality)

In the Neural Greenhouse, we do not take "I'm finished" as proof. We require a **Work Ledger**.

### Checkins & Checkouts
*   **Checkins:** The proposed plan. "I intend to plant a new neuroplasticity module."
*   **Checkouts:** The empirical result. "The module is planted, and the `neuro_flow_verified` fact is now `true`."

### The Audit Trail
The Fact Registry provides a live, factual map of the system. If the greenhouse "wilts" (a build fails), the Fact Registry tells you exactly which environmental condition changed.
